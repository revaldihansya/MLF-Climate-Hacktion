3. Why these upgrades?

The L1 model identifies variables associated with energy intensity, while the GA is restricted to variables that can plausibly be changed through building upgrades.

7. What does 8.85% mean?

It is a model-based counterfactual estimate — not a guaranteed engineering saving.

The GA asks: “If the building's feasible upgrade variables were changed, what would our trained energy-intensity model predict?” It does not directly simulate HVAC physics, construction cost, or measured post-retrofit consumption.

8. The takeaway

8.8% is a proof of concept, not the ceiling.

The result is constrained by historical CBECS data, limited GA search/sample size, and non-optimal predictive variables. Better data, stronger modelling and broader optimisation are the next steps toward the 25% ambition.
